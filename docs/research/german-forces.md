# German Order of Battle: Nijmegen Sector, 19–20 September 1944

Scenario window: morning 19 Sep 1944 (Guards Armoured Division meets the 82nd Airborne at Grave) to the evening of 20 Sep 1944 (Waal crossing and capture of both Nijmegen bridges).
Map: Grave (W) to Reichswald/German border (E); Maas/Mook (S) to Lent/Elst (N). About 1 km hexes, battalion counters.

Researched October 2026. This is a research draft and needs checking against the books in section 9 before the scenario data is final.

---

## 0. How to read this file

**Confidence labels** are given separately for Location (Loc), Strength (Str) and Equipment (Eq):

- **verified**: stated by at least one well-referenced source (official history, a source citing German war diaries, or a referenced battlefield study), with no serious conflict found.
- **provisional**: stated by one source only, by secondary or derivative sources, or sources disagree on the detail.
- **unknown**: no source found. Any figure shown is an *estimate* and the reasoning is given.

**Coordinates** are approximate (about ±300 m in town, about ±1 km for assembly areas), taken from modern maps of the named place. They are not taken from 1944 grid references.

**Clock times:** treat a one-hour gap between sources with care. From 17 Sep 1944 Britain was believed to be on BST (GMT+1), while German records used German summer time (GMT+2). A German "1700" may therefore be an Allied "1600". This was not checked source by source. For example, the 19 Sep attack is given as 1500 (MacDonald), 1600 (Saunders, Wikipedia) and about 1700 (Tieke).

**Source depth:** the most detailed German-side web source found is the Nafziger order of battle. It cites Kershaw's *It Never Snows in September* as its only source, so it is a derivative of Kershaw. The warhistory.org "10th SS Panzer Division at the Arnhem Battles" series follows Tieke's II SS-Panzerkorps history (*In the Firestorm of the Last Years of the War*), in a sometimes garbled English translation. Kershaw, Margry, Ryan and Harclerode were **not** read directly for this draft (see section 9).

## 2. Nijmegen garrison: start positions, morning 19 Sep

### 2.1 Kampfgruppe Henke (Army/Luftwaffe/Landesschützen mix)

Overall size: "approximately 750 primarily older men" plus flak (Tieke, via warhistory.org II). Saunders says "about 1,000 men" were in the city by late evening 17 Sep. That figure includes everyone, before the SS arrived.

Tieke's English translation lists "6 replacement battalions (3 companies each)". Nafziger, working from Kershaw, has "6 Ersatz Battalion (3 coys) (from Wehrkreis VI)" and Saunders has "three companies of trainees from Ersatz Battalion 6". The Tieke line is almost certainly a mistranslation of **Ersatz-Bataillon 6**: one battalion of three companies, not six battalions.

### 2.2 Kampfgruppe Euling and SS pioneers (Hunner Park / Valkhof, south end of the road bridge)

**Parent unit is disputed:**
- **II./SS-Panzergrenadier-Regiment 19** (9. SS-Pz.Div. "Hohenstaufen", attached to 10th SS): Wikipedia EN and NL, TracesOfWar's Nijmegen pages, and the wording of several battle histories.
- **I./SS-Panzergrenadier-Regiment 22** (10th SS "Frundsberg"): Euling's Knight's Cross biographies (TracesOfWar person page; Stenger Historica, which also mentions Rgt 21).
- Nafziger contradicts itself. It lists "SS Battalion Euling" under KG Reinhold, and also "Euling Panzer Battalion (22nd Panzer Grenadier Regiment)".
- **The brief's "II./SS-Pz.Gren.Rgt.22" matches neither main version.** Recommended scenario label: "Btl. Euling (SS-Pz.Gren.)", with the parent marked *provisional*. Either way it fought under 10th SS command.

**Groupings recommended for the game (Nijmegen):**
1. **G-EUL**: Euling battalion plus the Hunner Park AT guns (37/47/50 mm) as one strong infantry counter with an AT value.
2. **G-PIO**: SS pioneers at the Valkhof. Keep separate (engineer, "demolition" special ability) or merge into G-EUL if counter density is too high.
3. **G-JPZ**: one small tracked AT counter, 4 vehicles, type disputed. Or fold it into G-EUL as an AT bonus.
4. **G-HEN-1 / G-HEN-2**: two weak infantry counters, or one if the Landesschützen double count is real.
5. **G-FLAK-S / G-FLAK-N**: two static flak counters (high AT/AA value, zero movement).
6. **G-HEN-HQ**: Fort Hof van Holland, a fortified position counter.
7. **G-REI**: HQ plus a weak infantry counter at Lent.
8. **G-ART-S / G-ART-10**: off-board or north-bank artillery (indirect fire only).

## 3. Reinforcements via Pannerden and the Betuwe (north bank)

The Pannerden ferry (Pannerden is about 51.89, 6.04, **just off the east edge of the map**) and the Huissen ferry (MacDonald) were the only routes until the Arnhem road bridge was cleared on 21 Sep. On the ferry: the 10th SS pioneers (with Pionier-Kompanie Brandt & Munski, 18 Sep) built a 70-ton pontoon ferry. At first it carried only men, light vehicles and the remaining StuGs. A raft for heavy Panzers was finished PM 20 Sep, and Harmel said it "took my engineers more than two days". The first Panzers crossed "after nightfall" on 20 Sep for fear of air reconnaissance. Entry route into the map: Pannerden → Doornenburg → Bemmel → Ressen/Lent road, entering along the NE edge.

## 4. Reichswald front: Korps Feldt and II. Fallschirmkorps

### 4.1 Situation at the start (morning 19 Sep), after the failed 18 Sep attack

Strength figures for the 18 Sep attack force conflict sharply:
- **About 3,400 men**, "some mortars and artillery", in Kampfgruppen Stargaard, Fürstenberg, Greschick and Göbel (each "less than 1 bn"), plus a halftrack platoon (5 × 20 mm) (Nafziger/Kershaw).
- **About 2,300 men, five armoured cars, three halftracks with quad 20 mm and 24 medium mortars** (Saunders).
- **About four battalions and 500 combat effectives** in the 406th on D+2, 19 Sep (MacDonald, citing A Gp B sitreps). This is after the 18 Sep losses (at least 50 dead and 150 POW on 18 Sep per Saunders) and dispersal.

The game should use the **low, D+2 effective strength** for 19 Sep.

### 4.2 Attack forces for 20 Sep (arrived night 19/20, mostly II. Fallschirmkorps)

## 6. Bridge demolition: prepared, but not blown

- **Prepared:** yes, the road bridge certainly. 10th SS pioneers were tasked to prepare *both* Waal bridges (Tieke). After capture, Lt Jones (RE) cut the wires and found electrically fired TNT charges in green boxes, camouflaged in the structure, on a pier at the N end of the second span. He judged they were meant to drop a single span (Saunders ch.8). Tellermines were also found at the Lent roadblock. No source was found describing charges on the **railway** bridge being fired or found. Status: **unknown**.
- **Why not blown, in order of evidential weight:**
  1. **Model's prohibition.** He wanted the bridges for a counter-offensive. The A Gp B war diary shows authority to blow the road bridge was given only about 2330 on 20 Sep, after it had fallen (MacDonald, footnote 16). On 21 Sep A Gp B admitted demolition "would have been indicated" but said the local commander could not blow it on his own authority.
  2. **Harmel's account** (Saunders, Tieke, and he is widely quoted by Ryan and Kershaw): he ordered it blown on his own initiative as the tanks crossed, and the charges failed, "probably because the initiation cable had been cut by artillery fire". **Conflict:** this claim of independent action sits uneasily with the KTB. Treat it as Harmel's post-war version.
  3. **Gavin's view:** the bridge was assaulted from both ends, the demolition control point in the Nijmegen post office was destroyed (US troops held the post office from the night of 17 Sep), and Dutch underground fire hampered German work (MacDonald, footnote 17; Saunders).
  4. **Jan van Hoof:** a Dutch resistance member who may have cut the detonator wires on 18 Sep. He was executed on 19 Sep. A 1949 Dutch board accepted the possibility, but Allied records are vague and the claim is unproven. Saunders notes that engineers were checking and repairing the circuits on the bridge.
- Postscript, outside the window: Kampfschwimmer mined both bridges on the night of 28/29 Sep. The railway bridge's centre span dropped and the road bridge deck was holed.

**Game suggestion:** the road bridge can be demolished only after a German "authority" event (Model relents). The historical answer is "too late", so allow it from about turn 20 Sep 1900 at the earliest, with a high failure chance if Allied units hold the south bank or the post office, or have cleared Hunner Park. The railway bridge: no demolition option, or a low-probability one, because there is no evidence either way.

## 7. German supply situation

- **South-bank garrison (Euling, Henke's companies, south-bank flak):** supplied across the road and railway bridges from Lent. Nothing indicates a shortage of ammunition before the end. Once the N ends were lost (about 1800–1910, 20 Sep) the south-bank troops were **cut off**: surrenders on the railway bridge, Euling's breakout. **Game:** trace supply from S-bank German units across a bridge hex to the Lent/Betuwe road net.
- **North bank / Betuwe:** supplied by the **Pannerden ferry** (and Huissen, per MacDonald) from 18 Sep. Capacity was low at first: men, light vehicles and StuGs only. The heavy raft was ready PM 20 Sep and the first Panzers crossed after nightfall. KG Reinhold "lacked heavy weapons because of limited ferry traffic" (Tieke). **Game:** a supply source at the NE map edge (Bemmel road), with a limit on how many vehicles can enter per turn until the night of 20/21.
- **Reichswald:** rail to Kleve / Bedburg-Hau (Inf.Btl.526 came by train to Bedburg-Hau and marched), then foot. A Wehrmacht training area and ammunition dump lay in the woods by Groesbeek on 17 Sep (Groesbeek police report, Saunders ch.5). II. FJ-Korps units came by train and on foot from the Cologne area. Artillery and ammunition were thin: Saunders describes Göbel's group as lacking fire support, and the 526th had "no mortars". **Game:** supply source at the E map edge (Kranenburg road and the Reichswald), with low ammunition for 406th units.

## 10. Open questions and conflicts (priority order)

1. **Euling's battalion:** II./SS-PGR 19 (9th SS) or I./SS-PGR 22 (10th SS)? The brief's "II./SS-PGR 22" is unsupported.
2. **Hunner Park armour:** 4 × Jagdpanzer IV/70(V) (Nafziger) or 4 × "assault guns" (Saunders)? MacDonald's G-2 list mentions neither.
3. **Number and ownership of the 88s:** at least 5 are reported in different places, but one battery (4./572) had 4.
4. **Henke's Ersatz-Bataillon 6 and the 406th Landesschützen companies:** possibly the same men counted twice.
5. **Reichswald attack timing:** 0630 planned, 1000 actual (Saunders) or about 1100 (MacDonald). Beek retaken 20 Sep (Saunders) or 21 Sep (MacDonald).
6. **Which corps owned KG Becker:** Feldt tasking (Saunders) vs II. FJ-Korps troops. The axes of KG Greschick and KG Hermann (Saunders puts Hermann on Mook) need Kershaw to confirm.
7. **Tanks at Riethorst:** 3 Panthers (Saunders) or one tank (MacDonald).
8. **KG Knaust at Elst on 20 Sep 1600:** Nafziger only; it probably conflicts with the Arnhem bridge timeline.
