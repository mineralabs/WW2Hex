# Nijmegen sector, 17–18 September 1944 (campaign opening)

Research notes for the WW2Hex "Market Garden campaign" scenario, which runs from 15:00 on 17 Sep to
21:00 on 20 Sep 1944. This file covers the Nijmegen / Grave / Groesbeek / Mook / Reichswald sector
for **17 and 18 September**, so that the campaign can start at 15:00 on 17 Sep and reach the
positions already used by the 19–20 Sep scenario (`scenarios/nijmegen-1944`). It extends, and does
not replace, [allied-forces.md](allied-forces.md), [german-forces.md](german-forces.md) and
[crossings-timeline.md](crossings-timeline.md).

Researched 17 October 2026. **Status: provisional.** Nothing here should be marked `verified` in a
scenario file until the open questions in section 9 are checked against print sources.

## 0. How to read this file

- **Confidence labels**, in the order location / strength / equipment where three are given:
  - **V** (verified): two or more good sources agree.
  - **P** (provisional): one source, or an estimate whose basis is stated.
  - **U** (unknown).
- **Coordinates** are WGS84 decimal degrees, good to about ±300 m unless a wider figure is given.
  The "how" is stated each time:
  - *OSM*: the OpenStreetMap / Nominatim position of the named place.
  - *grid*: a 1944 six-figure grid reference converted with the fit in allied-forces.md section 6
    (lat = 51.826 + (N − 60.4)/111.2; lon = 5.808 + (E − 67.2)/68.88). It reproduces Kamp (763563)
    and Wyler (781583) to within about 200 m of OSM, but treat it as ±300–500 m.
  - *est*: my estimate from a written description; the description is quoted or cited.
- **Source keys** in square brackets are listed with URLs in section 10. (S) marks a secondary or
  web-only source.
- **"Estimate"** means a number I worked out; the basis is given beside it.
- **Unit ids** are those of `scenarios/nijmegen-1944/source/scenario.source.json` where the unit is
  the same, so the two can be merged. New ids are marked **NEW**.

### 0.1 Clock: all times are Allied "A" time (GMT+1)

- **Every time in this file is Allied "A" time, GMT+1.** British Double Summer Time ended on
  17 Sep 1944, so British and US units in Holland were on GMT+1 from the start of Market (P: general
  reference; consistent with every Allied source used, which put the 82nd's drops at 13:00–13:45).
- **German records and the Dutch civil clock used German summer time, GMT+2.** German-clock times
  (Tieke, Harmel, Feldt, Rasch, Schwappacher, Dutch local accounts) have been **converted one hour
  earlier** and are marked "(G hh:mm)" with the original beside them.
- Where a secondary author (chiefly Saunders) quotes a time from a German account without saying
  which clock, I have assumed German time and say so. These are flagged.
- **Light** (computed from standard solar formulas for 51.84 N, 5.86 E; estimate): sunrise about
  06:15 and sunset about 18:45 A on 17–20 Sep. Darkness therefore covers campaign turns 3 (19:00)
  to 8 (05:00) on each night, with turns 2 and 8/9 at dusk and dawn.

## 1. Summary for scenario design

1. **At 15:00 on 17 Sep the whole 82nd Airborne first lift is on the ground.** It dropped and landed
   between 13:08 and about 14:00 A: 7,277 parachutists (CMH; Warren about 7,250; Saunders 7,467
   including gliders), the 376th PFA Bn with 12 pack howitzers, Battery A of the 80th AA (AT) Bn with
   8 × 57-mm, and about 105 men of Browning's I Airborne Corps HQ [CMH7; W97; SAUN-ch5]. All nine
   parachute battalions are assembled (about 90%) and **moving off their zones** toward their
   objectives at the start. Nothing on the Allied side is yet "in position" at 15:00 (section 2).
2. **Key Allied events, 17 Sep:** Grave bridge taken (south end by E/504 in the afternoon; "Bridge 11
   is ours" reported 16:50); Heumen lock bridge taken intact about 18:00; Malden and Hatert bridges
   blown in the late afternoon and at 19:40; Mook railway bridge blown; Groesbeek taken about 15:00;
   1/508 sent into Nijmegen from about 20:00–20:30 and stopped around the Keizer Karelplein from about
   22:00 (section 3).
3. **Key events, 18 Sep:** Honinghutje (Bridge 10) partly blown at 10:30 and taken about 11:00–12:00;
   G/508 gets within about 100–400 yd of the road bridge in the morning and is withdrawn at 14:00;
   Korps Feldt overruns parts of LZ T and DZ/LZ N from about 08:00 and is thrown off by 1/508
   (13:00–14:00) and C/505 (12:40–13:50) just before the glider lift; the glider lift lands from
   14:31 (319th, 320th, 456th FA Bns, B/80th AA, Div Arty HQ); the B-24 drop lands from about 15:57 to
   16:30 (section 4).
4. **German side:** at 15:00 on 17 Sep Nijmegen held only a weak rear-area garrison under Oberst
   Henke (about 750–1,000 men by late evening, with one heavy flak battery). SS-Pz.Aufkl.Abt. 9
   (Gräbner) passed through on the evening of 17 Sep and left a few armoured cars. **Euling's
   battalion and Baumgärtel's SS pioneers reached the road bridge in the morning or about midday of
   18 Sep** via the Pannerden ferry, and **Reinhold** followed in the early afternoon. Korps Feldt's
   406th Division attacked the landing zones on 18 Sep with four weak Kampfgruppen (about 2,300 men
   per Saunders, 3,400 per Nafziger) and failed (section 5).
5. **Designer decisions** are collected in section 8.4. The biggest are: how to script bridge
   demolitions (Grave, canal bridges, Mook, Honinghutje); whether to split the 82nd's battalions
   into detached companies (D/508 on LZ T, E/504 south of the Maas, Adams's platoon in the post
   office); how to bring the SS into Nijmegen on 18 Sep at the right time from an edge 15–20 km
   away; and whether to script the German losses of 18 Sep or let play decide them.

### 3.4 The 508th's first attempt on the Nijmegen road bridge, night 17/18 Sep

- **Who:** A and B Cos, 1/508 (Lt Col Shields Warren), with battalion HQ; a platoon of C Co and the
  S-2 section went in "by a different route and at an earlier hour" (Warren's 18:30 patrol). C Co
  less one platoon stayed at De Ploeg [508R-1b; CMH7].
- **When:** Gavin learned about 18:00 that 1/508 had not moved on the bridge [SAUN-ch5]. Departure
  20:00 (Saunders) or 20:30 (508R, Warren). A Co reached the rendezvous south of Nijmegen at 19:00 and
  B Co got lost (CMH; CMH times are possibly the Div log). First contact about 22:00 at the Keizer
  Karelplein (CMH "about two hours before midnight"; Saunders "about 22:00 hours ... briefed and
  dispatched" conflicts). Warren: "reached Nijmegen at 00:15 on the 18th" [CMH7; W97; SAUN-ch7].
- **How far:**
  - Saunders and CMH: stalled at the Keizer Karelplein when an SS convoy (half-tracks and trucks)
    arrived and counter-attacked. CMH thinks this was the advance guard of the 9th SS Recon Bn.
  - Warren: "penetrated to within 400 yards of the bridge before the Germans closed in".
  - 508R: A Co "had reached and was fighting in the parkway at 716625" (grid = 51.845, 5.872, the
    Keizer Traianusplein / Hunner Park end); B Co pushed through A's position to 708621 (= Keizer
    Karelplein) by daylight, and "remained in possession of the park at 716625" into the morning.
  - **Reconciliation (P):** fighting centred on the Keizer Karelplein, with parties reaching the
    Oranjesingel parkway toward the Hunner Park. For the game, the 1/508 counter reaches the Keizer
    Karelplein hex and no further on turns 5–9.
- **Post office:** Capt Jonathan Adams with a platoon of A Co destroyed what was believed to be the
  demolition control room and was cut off there until 22 Sep [CMH7; 508R-1b]. Gavin later doubted it
  was a real control point (crossings-timeline.md). **Not a counter:** at most a "post office held"
  marker that affects the bridge demolition rule in german-forces.md section 6.
- **End:** before dawn a German counter-attack stopped a new attack. At 08:00 on 18 Sep the battalion
  was ordered back to clear the landing zone; A Co and the 81-mm mortars returned to De Ploeg; B Co

### 8.4 Decisions for the scenario designer

1. **Bridge demolitions on turns 1–11.** Historically: Grave intact; Heumen intact; Malden blown about
   turn 1; Mook railway bridge blown turn 1–2; Hatert blown turn 3; Honinghutje rail blown turn 10
   and road bridge damaged (no tanks). Script these as fixed events, or give each a demolition chance
   that rises when Allied units come adjacent. The road bridge rule in german-forces.md section 6
   (no demolition before the authority event) still applies.
2. **Company detachments.** E/504 (south end of Grave bridge), D/508 (LZ T), F/508 (to 3/508 at turn
   14) and Adams's post-office platoon. Either use small counters or fold them into their battalions
   and accept small position errors.
3. **SS arrival timing.** Entering Euling at the Pannerden edge on turn 9 gets him to the bridge about
   turn 11, as historically, but only if his movement rate allows 20 km in two turns. Otherwise
   enter him nearer (at Bemmel) or earlier.
4. **Gräbner** is a "visiting" unit: enters turn 3, must leave by turn 6. It needs an exit rule or a
   scripted withdrawal event.
5. **Feldt's 18 Sep attack.** Either enter the four groups at full strength on turn 8 and let play
   decide the losses, or script a strength reduction to the existing 19 Sep values (250 each) at
   turn 16. The historical German loss on 18 Sep was at least 50 dead and about 150–200 PW, plus 16
   × 20-mm guns; the rest of the drop to 500 effectives was dispersal.
6. **Glider lift and B-24 drop on turn 13** need the drop/landing zones to be clear; historically the
   82nd had to clear them by about 14:00. If the Germans still hold LZ T or N on turn 13, the
   designer must decide what happens (losses, diversion to LZ O, as half of one serial did).
7. **Allied HQs** (Div HQ, Corps HQ, Div Arty HQ) are optional counters.
8. **57-mm guns:** keep `a-80-aa-a` and `a-80-aa-b` as counters, or attach their guns to the
   battalions as the existing scenario does.
9. **Night.** The existing scenario does not model night. 17/18 and 18/19 Sep are mostly night turns
   (3–8, 15–20); the 1/508 attack into Nijmegen was a night attack.
10. **Turn alignment** with the existing 19 Sep scenario (section 0.2 and 8.3).

## 9. Open questions and conflicts

1. **Grave bridge capture time.** Warren's 16:50 is the battalion's report; CMH says the bridge was
   taken within three hours of the drop (by about 16:15). The exact moment the south end fell is not
   recorded in what I read.
2. **Which 1/504 company went to Bridges 8 and 9.** Warren: A Co to 8, C Co to 9. Saunders: C Co to 8,
   A Co to 9. The 504th after-action report and van Lunteren would settle it.
3. **Malden (8) blown time.** Only Wikipedia (16:15).
4. **1/508's departure time and how far it got** (20:00 or 20:30; Keizer Karelplein or the parkway by
   Hunner Park, grid 716625). Check the 1/508 S-3 journal and Nordyke.
5. **The SS convoy at the Keizer Karelplein** about 22:00: Gräbner's advance guard (CMH) or another
   10th SS element? Kershaw and Tieke should say.
6. **Euling and Reinhold arrival times** (Tieke "midday" together; Nafziger Euling early AM,
   Reinhold early PM at the ferry). The exact clock time is not in any source read.
7. **Route and time of Ersatz-Btl. 6** into Nijmegen on 17 Sep.
8. **Korps Feldt's axes on 18 Sep**: which of Stargaard, Fürstenberg, Greschick and Göbel attacked
   where, and the strength of each (2,300 vs 3,400). Kershaw, MS B-368 and the 406th records quoted
   by Saunders (Feldt, Rasch) are the sources to check.
9. **Clock of the Saunders 06:30 / 10:30 times** for the 18 Sep attack (assumed German here).
10. **The 18 Sep glider lift by unit**: which serials carried the 456th, how many guns each battalion
    lost among the "six guns" outside the lines, and whether the 320th's Gennep groups were all 320th
    men. The 82nd G-3 journal and the ww2gp serial list would settle it.
11. **The 319th's gun type** (75-mm pack or 105-mm M3), carried over from allied-forces.md.
12. **Henke's HQ**: Nebo monastery (Tieke) or Fort Hof van Holland (Saunders), and when it moved.
13. **DZ T position**: the 508th's grid 765585 is about 1.3 km north-west of the zone as Warren
    describes it. A 1944 operations map would settle it.
14. **Heumen lock bridge coordinates** differ by about 700 m between two existing research files.
15. **British clock change on 17 Sep 1944** (end of Double Summer Time) is from general knowledge and
    should be confirmed from an official source.
