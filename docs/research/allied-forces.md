# Allied forces: Nijmegen sector, 19–20 September 1944

Research notes for the WW2Hex Nijmegen scenario. The scenario runs from the morning of 19 Sep 1944
(Guards Armoured reaches the 82nd Airborne at Grave) to the evening of 20 Sep 1944 (Waal crossing and
capture of the Nijmegen bridges). The map runs from Grave (west) to the Reichswald (east), and from
Mook and the Maas (south) to Lent and Elst (north). Hexes are about 1 km and counters are battalions.

**Status of this file: provisional.** No unit here should be marked `verified` in a scenario file
until the open questions at the end are checked against the print sources.

## How to read this file

- **Confidence labels.** Each unit has three labels, in the order location / strength / equipment:
  - **V** (verified): two or more good sources agree.
  - **P** (provisional): one source, or an estimate whose basis is stated.
  - **U** (unknown).
- **Coordinates** are WGS84 decimal degrees and good to roughly ±300 m. Most were worked out from
  OpenStreetMap place positions, or from 1944 grid references converted with a fit calibrated on two
  known points (see the note under "Coordinates"). They are not quoted from the sources.
- **Source keys** in square brackets, such as [CMH8], are listed with their URLs under "Sources" at
  the end. Keys marked (S) are secondary.
- **"Estimate"** means I worked the number out myself, and the basis is given beside it. Every other
  number comes from a cited source.
- **Establishment and actual strength.**
  - "Establishment" is the official table of organisation (T/O or WE).
  - "Actual" is a strength reported at the time.
  - If only the establishment is known, the file says so.

## 1. Summary for scenario design

1. **On the map at the start (morning 19 Sep).** All of the US 82nd Airborne Division except the
   325th Glider Infantry: nine parachute battalions, four artillery battalions, the engineers, and two
   anti-tank batteries of the 80th AA Bn. No British ground unit is on the map at dawn.
2. **British reinforcements on 19 Sep.** They enter at the Grave bridge:
   - 2nd Household Cavalry Regiment (B Sqn) at about 08:20.
   - Grenadier Guards Group: tanks at Grave about 10:00, south of Nijmegen by 11:00.
   - Coldstream Guards Group: during the day; attached to the 82nd as divisional reserve.
   - Irish Guards Group: harboured at Malden on the evening of 19 Sep.
   - Welsh Guards Group: south-west of Nijmegen by the night of 19/20 Sep.
   - Guards divisional artillery: in action from the afternoon of 19 Sep.
3. **On 20 Sep.** The Sherwood Rangers Yeomanry (8th Armoured Brigade) came under 82nd command. This
   is provisional, from a single source (Saunders).
4. **Not available in the scenario window:**
   - 325th GIR: weathered out every day from 19 to 22 Sep and landed on 23 Sep.
   - The rest of the 80th AA Bn (Batteries C–F): arrived 23 Sep.
   - 43rd (Wessex) Division: reached Nijmegen about nightfall on 21 Sep.
   - Corps medium artillery (64 Medium Regt): arrived 21 Sep.
5. **Waal crossing assets.** 26 British canvas assault boats arrived about 14:30–14:40 on 20 Sep;
   32 or 33 had been promised. 3/504 crossed first, then 1/504, with C Company of the 307th Engineers
   paddling. Two squadrons of 2nd Irish Guards fired from the south bank, and about 100 US and British
   field guns supported.
6. **Supply.** The 82nd was critically short of food and ammunition on 19–20 Sep:
   - The 19 Sep air drop largely failed (about 20% recovered).
   - The large 20 Sep drop (310 aircraft, 441 tons) only arrived from 16:48.
   - No supplies reached the division by road before 20 Sep.

## 2. US 82nd Airborne Division (Brig. Gen. James M. Gavin)

Side: allied. Parent: I Airborne Corps (Browning), First Allied Airborne Army.

### 2.1 Establishments used for estimates

- **Parachute infantry battalion, T/O 7-36/7-37 (17 Feb 1942, with Change 1 of 24 Feb 1944)**
  [BS-PIB44a]. This is probably the table in force in Sep 1944; the 1 Aug 1944 revision may not yet
  have been applied.
  - Strength: **530 all ranks**.
  - HQ Company: light machine-gun (LMG) platoon with **8 LMGs**; 81-mm mortar platoon with
    **4 × 81-mm mortars**.
  - Three rifle companies, each of three platoons. Each platoon has 2 rifle squads of 12 men, a
    60-mm mortar squad, and 2 "optional" LMGs.
  - Battalion totals: **18 rifle squads, 9 × 60-mm mortars, 44 LMGs, 21 bazookas (2.36-in)**.
  - The Aug 1944 table [BS-PIB44b] has 583 all ranks and the same weapon layout.
  - In practice the 82nd's battalions jumped overstrength. See the actual figures for the 505th below.
- **Parachute field artillery battalion:** **12 × 75-mm pack howitzers M1A1** in 3 batteries
  [FAJ2000]. The 376th jumped with 544 men [FAJ2000; W97].
- **Glider field artillery battalion:** 2 firing batteries of 6 guns, so **12 guns** (P) [WWA-320].
  The gun type is disputed; see 2.5.
- **80th Airborne AA (AT) Bn:**
  - Batteries A–C were anti-tank, each with **8 × 57-mm AT guns**. Battery A had 86 men, 8 × 57-mm
    and 9 jeeps [W97].
  - Batteries D–F were anti-aircraft, with .50-cal MGs.
  - Some sources say the AT batteries had 37-mm guns. 57-mm is supported for Holland by Warren.

### 2.2 Division-level strength

- **Jumped on 17 Sep:** 7,277 men (CMH ch. 7). Warren gives about 7,250, and Saunders 7,467 for the
  first lift [CMH7; W97; SAUN].
- **Jump strengths by unit** [W97]:
  - 505th PIR: 2,151.
  - 504th PIR: 2,031 aboard; 3 refused and 2 wounded flew back.
  - 508th PIR: 1,922.
  - 307th Engineers: 388 on DZ N.
  - 376th PFAB: 544 with 12 guns.
- **505th battalions at 22:00 on 17 Sep** [W97]:
  - 1/505: 43 officers and 614 men.
  - 2/505: 42 officers and 552 men.
  - 3/505: 40 officers and 592 men.
- **Losses:**
  - The 505th lost 63 men on 18 Sep [CMH7].
  - By the end of 19 Sep the division had 649 casualties in hospital at Groesbeek and over 150 dead
    [W97].
- **18 Sep glider lift:** 454 gliders, of which 385 landed inside 82nd lines [W97]. CMH gives "450".
  - Loads by the division's figures: 1,899 troops, 206 jeeps, 123 trailers, 60 guns.
  - Loads by the troop carriers' figures: 1,779 troops, 201 jeeps, 55 guns. Six guns landed outside
    the division area.
  - Units carried: the 319th, 320th and 456th FA Bns, B/80th AA Bn, and Div Arty HQ [W97; WWGP-S].

**Command conflicts.**
- *1/504.* Wikipedia lists "Lt Col John T. Berry". CMH, the H Company report and the 508th order of
  battle all give Maj Harrison, so use Harrison.
- *3/505.* Wikipedia's entry is garbled. Krause commanded the battalion in Normandy; Kaiser
  commanded it in Holland [508R order of battle; TOW snippet].
- *2/508.* Wikipedia gives "Shanley, then Holmes". The 508th's December 1944 report gives Holmes for
  Holland.
- *Ranks.* CMH gives Cook and Wellems as Majors; Wikipedia gives Lt Col.

#### What each infantry battalion did on 19–20 Sep

**1/504**
- 19 Sep: guarded the canal bridges. On 19 Sep (CMH) part of the 504th relieved 2/508 at Hatert.
  Phillips says three 504th companies were left guarding the Grave, Heumen and Mook bridges
  [CMH8; PHIL].
- 20 Sep: moved to the crossing site and crossed after 3/504. Cleared Fort Hof van Holland, which
  became the regimental CP, and took over the north end of the railway bridge. One platoon of about
  25 men was attached to H/504 at the road bridge [CMH8; HCO].

**2/504**
- Held the Grave area until the Welsh Guards relieved it [SAUN].
- 20 Sep: cleared around the PGEM power station and gave fire support, including 81-mm mortars, for
  the crossing [SAUN; NOV3](S).

**3/504**
- 19 Sep: crossed the Heumen bridge in the evening and harboured at Jonkerbos at 21:15 [WIKI-N](S).
- 20 Sep: warning order 09:00; at the dike 14:00; boats arrived 14:30; first wave launched 14:57,
  H-hour 15:00.
  - First wave: H and I Cos and the battalion command group. Second wave: G Co.
  - CMH: 13 of 26 boats returned, and 11 made six crossings.
  - A platoon of H Co took Fort Hof van Holland.
  - Reached the north end of the road bridge at 19:38 by the H Co report; CMH gives 19:10
    [HCO; CMH8].

**1/505**
- 19 Sep: quiet, apart from strafing.
- 20 Sep: German attack from about 10:00–11:00 [SAUN; CMH8].
  - Riethorst held at first, with ten 75-mm howitzers in support, then the defenders fell back to
    the Kiekberg in mid-afternoon.
  - Mook outposts were overrun by 15:00 [CMH8]. Saunders says Mook was "almost in German hands" by
    14:10.
  - The reserve (two A Co platoons) and **six Coldstream tanks** retook Mook by nightfall.
  - Losses on 20 Sep: 20 killed, 54 wounded, 7 missing [CMH8].

**2/505**
- 19 Sep: left the Groesbeek area at 12:45 [PHIL].
  - D Co rode British tanks toward the railway bridge at 15:00 and was stopped [CMH8].
  - E and F Cos attacked the road bridge with the Grenadiers from about 16:00 and were stopped at the
    Hunner Park / Keizer Lodewijkplein traffic circle [CMH8; PHIL].
- 20 Sep:
  - A 05:30 attack failed [PHIL].
  - Combined assault with the Grenadiers took Hunner Park and the traffic circle: CMH gives 16:20,
    Phillips 14:45.
  - South end of the road bridge cleared about 18:00 [PHIL].

**3/505**
- Held Groesbeek.
- 20 Sep: the German 526th Bn attacked through De Horst toward Groesbeek at 08:00 and was broken up
  by machine-gun fire [SAUN].

**1/508**
- 19 Sep:
  - The G Co platoon on Devil's Hill was driven off three times in the morning.
  - A Co retook the crest by 18:00, losing 10 killed and 7 wounded.
  - B Co took Wyler, held by about one German company, at the same time [508R].
- 20 Sep:
  - A dawn attack on Devil's Hill was repulsed.
  - Wyler: artillery from 09:30; a two-company attack at 09:50 was broken up; then a battalion-strength
    attack. The two platoons there withdrew from Wyler [508R; CMH8].
- **CMH error:** CMH calls the Devil's Hill company "Company A, 505th"; the 508th's own report shows
  it was A/508.

**2/508**
- Relieved by the 504th on 19 Sep (CMH; the 508th report may place it on 20 Sep).
- 20 Sep: moved to the Kamp–Voxhil line, about 51.788, 5.940 to 51.805, 5.945 [508R; CMH8].

**3/508**
- 19 Sep: set roadblocks on the Nijmegen–Kleve road ("Highway K") at Berg en Dal and Beek.
- 20 Sep, late afternoon: an armour and infantry attack drove the platoon out of Beek (CMH: battalion
  strength). H Co re-established the position at 21:40, but was partly surrounded overnight. Beek was
  retaken on 21 Sep [508R; CMH8].

### 2.6 325th Glider Infantry Regiment: not available

- **Planned:** to land on 19 Sep (D+2). The mission was raised from 209 to 219 gliders, due at 10:00.
- **What happened:**
  - 19 Sep: postponed for low cloud over the Grantham bases. A few aircraft took off and were
    recalled.
  - Grounded again on 20 and 21 Sep; no missions flew on 22 Sep [W97].
  - **Landed 23 Sep on LZ O, 16:03–17:17:** 406 Wacos carrying 3,385 troops (325th GIR, 4 batteries
    of the 80th AA Bn, two engineer companies, the Recon and MP Platoons), 104 jeeps, 59 trailers and
    25 guns. The 325th assembled at 75% strength and moved to Groesbeek [W97; WWGP-S; CMH8].
- **Effect:** the Coldstream Guards Group stood in as divisional reserve. CMH says it stayed until
  23 Sep; the 1st Coldstream war diary says it was released on 22 Sep [CMH8; SAUN].
- **Units:** 1/325 (Lt Col Klemm Boyd, then Lt Col Teddy Sanford); 2/325 (Maj Charles Major);
  2/401st GIR (Maj Osmund Leahy) [WIKI-OOB](S).
- **For the game:** leave it out of the 19–20 Sep scenario. A "what if the weather had cleared"
  variant could bring it in on LZ O or LZ N at about 10:00–11:00 on 19 Sep.

## 3. Guards Armoured Division (Maj Gen Allan Adair) and attached British units

Side: allied. Parent: XXX Corps (Lt Gen Brian Horrocks), Second Army.

**Organisation.**
- The division fought as four regimental groups, each pairing an armoured battalion with an
  infantry battalion of the same regiment [BMH-GAD](S).
- 5th Guards Armoured Brigade (Brig Norman Gwatkin) usually commanded the Grenadier and Irish groups.
  The 2nd Grenadier Guards war diary has the Grenadier group transferring to 32 Guards Brigade only on
  22 Sep.
- 32nd Guards Brigade (Brig George Johnson) commanded the Coldstream and Welsh groups.
- Saunders' order of battle allocates the groups differently. I think it is wrong.

**At dawn on 19 Sep no Guards unit was on the map.** The Zon Bailey bridge opened about 06:15 on
19 Sep, and the Grenadier group led north through St Oedenrode, Veghel, Uden and Grave
[GG2-WD; IG3-WD; TM3](S for TM3). All British units below are **reinforcements**. Their arrival times
are given in the tables.

### 3.1 Establishments used for estimates (all P)

- **Armoured regiment / battalion, 1944** (secondary; check against the War Establishment tables):
  - RHQ: 4 tanks.
  - 3 squadrons (Guards numbering No. 1–3), each of a squadron HQ (2–4 tanks) and 4 troops of 4.
    Each troop had 3 × 75-mm Sherman and 1 × Sherman Firefly (17-pdr).
  - Recce troop: 11–12 Stuarts.
  - Totals: about **58–61 medium tanks, of which about 12 were Fireflies, plus about 11 Stuarts**
    [QMS; MIN](S).
  - The Guards' Shermans were mostly Sherman V (M4A4) and Sherman VC Firefly. That is general
    knowledge, not confirmed for these battalions. The Noviomagus account calls three of the
    road-bridge tanks "Sherman IV".
- **Armoured reconnaissance battalion (2nd Welsh Guards):** same layout with Cromwells, and A30
  Challengers (17-pdr) in the troops in place of Fireflies [SAUN; MIN](S).
- **Motor battalion, WE II/231/3 (Jan 1944)** [BS-MOT44]:
  - **854 all ranks.**
  - 3 motor companies, each of 3 motor platoons plus a scout platoon with 11 carriers.
  - Support company: 3 anti-tank platoons (**12 × 6-pdr**) and 2 MMG platoons (**8 Vickers MMG**).
  - Battalion totals: **87 LMGs (Bren), 30 PIATs, 30 × 2-in mortars, 6 × 3-in mortars**.
  - 1st Grenadier Guards companies are named King's, No. 2, No. 3 and No. 4. Whether No. 4 was a
    support company, or a non-standard fourth motor company, is unverified.
- **Infantry battalion, WE II/233/2 (1943, in force 1944)** [BS-INF43]:
  - **845 all ranks**, in 4 rifle companies (12 platoons, **36 rifle sections**).
  - **63 Brens, 23 PIATs, 26 × 2-in mortars, 6 × 3-in mortars, 6 × 6-pdr**, 13 carriers in the
    carrier platoon.
  - This applies to 5th Coldstream, 3rd Irish and 1st Welsh Guards, the lorried battalions of
    32nd Guards Brigade.
- **Field regiment RA:** 3 batteries, **24 × 25-pdr** (P, standard establishment). Personnel about
  600+ (estimate).
- **Anti-tank regiment RA (armoured division):** sources differ.
  - A hobby source gives 4 batteries: 2 SP (M10 17-pdr) and 2 towed 17-pdr, about 48 guns
    (unverified).
  - ra39-45 lists only Q, Y and Z batteries for the 21st [RA-21].
- **Armoured car regiment (2nd HCR):** squadrons of 5 troops (2 Daimler armoured cars and 2 Daimler
  Dingo scout cars each), plus a support troop and a heavy troop. The 2nd HCR heavy troops used AEC
  armoured cars [NDNG](S). No vehicle counts were found.

#### What the Guards units did on 19–20 Sep

**2nd HCR**
- 19 Sep:
  - B Sqn went on by the Sofiaweg to Berg en Dal and Ubbergen to support the 508th, firing on Beek
    and Wyler [NOV3](S).
  - 11:45, XXX Corps log: "HCR report enemy beaten off high ground NE of GROESBEEK" [SAUN].
  - Cars screened the Waal and the city's flanks. Their 2-pdrs drove the crews off two 88s at Lent
    [SAUN].

**Grenadier group (2nd and 1st Grenadier Guards)**
- **19 Sep attack.** H-hour 16:00 [SAUN], 16:30 [GG2-WD] or 17:00 from Sionshof [NOV3]. Three
  forces:
  1. **Railway bridge:**
     - Saunders: 1 troop of No. 3 Sqn (5 tanks, 3 left afterwards), 1 platoon of No. 2 Coy 1st Gren,
       and D Co 2/505.
     - Noviomagus: 5 Shermans, 2 knocked out.
     - CMH: D/505 on "five British tanks and five other armored vehicles"; the lead tank was knocked
       out; stopped by fire from the marshalling yards and an 88.
  2. **Road bridge:**
     - Saunders: 3 troops of No. 3 Sqn, 3 platoons of No. 2 Coy, and E and F Cos 2/505.
     - Three tanks lost on 19 Sep (Lt Moller's troop). CMH: the lead tank plus three more were hit.
     - Stopped at Hunner Park.
  3. **Post office:**
     - 4 Shermans of No. 1 Sqn, 2 platoons of No. 3 Coy 1st Gren, and a platoon of 2/505
       (Maj George Thorne).
     - Took the post office about 17:30; it held no bridge detonators.
     - A sortie at 20:00 lost its lead tank to an SP gun [NOV3](S).
  - War diary: "heavy opposition encountered 400 yards from both bridges. Consolidated for the night"
    [GG2-WD].
- **20 Sep.**
  - From 08:00–08:30, 1st Grenadiers cleared western Nijmegen and towards Kelfkensbos; Lt Prescott's
    troop of No. 2 Sqn supported, and his tank was lost.
  - At 15:30 the Valkhof and Hunner Park assault began [SAUN]:
    - King's Coy attacked the Valkhof.
    - No. 4 Coy (Maj Stanley) attacked Hunner Park and the Belvedere.
    - No. 2 and No. 3 Coys covered the flanks.
  - CMH: traffic circle and Hunner Park taken at 16:20. War diary: "vicinity of bridge eventually
    reached at 1700 hrs".
  - **Road bridge:**
    - War diary: "At 1900 hrs a Tp of tanks was successfully rushed across the bridge encountering at
      least one 88 mm ... losing two tanks, one only temporary ... Bridge consolidated by 2200 hrs".
    - CMH: 19:10. Robinson's first move by his watch: 18:13 [SAUN]. Tank Museum: link-up 19:15.
    - Tanks:
      - Sgt Robinson's troop of No. 1 Sqn (Maj Trotter; Capt Lord Carrington following).
      - Noviomagus lists tanks 25–28: Sgts Pacey, Robinson, Billingham and Knight.
      - CMH says "Three tanks reached the far end"; Saunders says 5 set out and 2 reached Lent at first.

**Coldstream group (1st and 5th Coldstream)**
- 19 Sep: attached to the 82nd as divisional reserve [CMH8; SAUN].
- 20 Sep: about 6 tanks of No. 3 Sqn, 1st Coldstream, with two 1/505 platoons retook Mook by
  nightfall [CMH8; SAUN]. Earlier a single Coldstream tank at the Mook rail underpass lost a track on a
  US mine and was abandoned. 5th Coldstream then pushed the Germans back toward Riethorst [SAUN].

**Irish group (2nd and 3rd Irish Guards)**
- **20 Sep, Waal crossing.** Fitzgerald's Irish Guards history, quoted by Saunders: "At half-past two
  the tanks moved slowly up to the river, reaching their positions just before three. No. 2 Squadron

  land round the factory."
  - They fired HE and AP at Fort Hof van Holland, laid smoke, and ran low on ammunition.
  - The 3rd Irish Guards diary confirms "tks of 2 (Armd) IG" supported the crossing [IG3-WD].
  - Giles Vandeleur watched from the PGEM building with Browning and Horrocks [RYAN].
- **Conflict on where 3rd Irish Guards spent the night of 20 Sep.** Its diary says: "As darkness
  fell, the Bn group moved forward to harbour the night on the NORTH bank of the R. WAAL." CMH says no
  armoured group crossed until 21 Sep [IG3-WD; CMH8].

**Welsh group (2nd and 1st Welsh Guards)**
- Divisional reserve south-west of Nijmegen and around Grave.
- 20 Sep, about 17:30: sent to Molenhoek to back up the Mook fight [NOV3](S).

### 5.2 Supply status and ground supply

- **Need.** The 82nd planned 265 tons a day by air for the first three days, after which it was to be
  supplied by road. Its real need was about 300 tons a day, including about 215 tons of munitions
  [CL-PARA](S).
- **20 Sep.** "The supply dumps of the 82d were running low, and it had not yet received any
  supplies by road" [W97]. The first truck convoys had only reached the Meuse–Escaut canal.
- **British supply arrangements.**
  - The US airborne divisions were to be kept supplied from British stocks for common items such as
    fuel and rations. 161 Forward Maintenance Centre at Bourg-Léopold was earmarked for them
    [BTN-SUP](S; page now offline).
  - The road corridor was congested, with no spare lift for the 82nd. On 20 Sep the division had no
    trucks to distribute supplies, and Ridgway asked for British transport [CL-PARA](S).
- **Ammunition.**
  - The 376th PFAB was rationed to 5 rounds per mission at Mook [WHN-Mook](S).
  - The 320th GFAB asked for "all they can get" on 19 Sep [CL-PARA](S).
  - The divisional and Guards guns on 20 Sep had about 50 rounds per gun [SAUN].
  - A 75-mm shortage from 22 Sep was eased with rounds from the British 1st Airborne Division's
    seaborne stock [CL-PARA](S).
- **Dutch help.** Dutch civilians collected resupply bundles and shared food [CMH8; W97].
- **Corridor.** The road was cut around Veghel on 22 Sep and reopened at about 18:00 on 23 Sep
  [W97]. That is outside this window, but it explains why the reinforcements stopped.
- **Guards Armoured fuel and ammunition.** No figures were found for 19–20 Sep. The 21st Army Group
  administrative report at King's College London is the likely source [KCL-PYMAN].
- **Glider pilots as infantry.**
  - CMH says the two US divisions put about 450 glider pilots into the line [CMH8].
  - Warren says 185 were sent to Mook on the night of 20 Sep. He also argues that the 82nd could not
    have mustered 450 before 23 Sep [W97].
  - Most were collected at the division CP for guard and supply duty.

## 7. Open questions and conflicts to resolve

1. **Road bridge crossing.** The time is 18:30, 19:00, 19:10 or 19:15. Two, three or four tanks
   reached the north end. Check the 2nd Grenadier Guards war diary (WO 171/1254), Forbes's regimental
   history, and Margry's *Market Garden Then and Now*.
2. **The 19 Sep attack.** H-hour is 16:00, 16:30 or 17:00. The tank counts per column also differ
   between Saunders and Noviomagus.
3. **Irish Guards tanks at the crossing:** 10, 24 or about 30. "Two squadrons" is the best-supported
   description.
4. **Boats.** 32 or 33 were promised and 26 arrived. The carrying unit is given as the Guards' field
   park squadron, the 43rd Division, or the corps engineer train.
5. **Gun type of the 319th and 320th Glider FA Bns** (75-mm pack or 105-mm M3) and their actual gun
   counts on 19 Sep.
6. **Battalion strengths on 19 Sep** for the 504th and 508th, and for all Guards battalions. The
   82nd G-1 periodic reports and the Guards war diaries (WO 171) should have them.
7. **Coldstream group.** Its arrival time and reserve area on 19 Sep (Sionshof, or west of Groesbeek).
   Release from 82nd command on 22 Sep (war diary) or 23 Sep (CMH).
8. **Whether 3rd Irish Guards harboured north of the Waal on the night of 20 Sep.**
9. **Sherwood Rangers arrival time on 20 Sep**, and whether "Royals" means the Royal Dragoons or
   the 2nd HCR.
10. **XXX Corps Tac HQ and I Airborne Corps HQ** on 19–20 Sep: Malden, or the Groesbeek woods.
11. **Position of 153 Field Regt on 20 Sep:** the Goffertpark, or about 1,000 yd west of the
    railway bridge.
12. **The PGEM launch site and Fort Hof van Holland** coordinates need checking on a 1944 map.
